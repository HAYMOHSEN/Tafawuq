## Residual doubts
1. APP-LEVEL (not fixable inside a unit file, affects the trust in every grammar key): `U.closeMatch` (js/core.js) + `grade()` (js/quiz.js) accept ANY answer one edit away from a key longer than 6 characters, including grammatical errors. Reproduced with the real grader: "It has been raining" for "had been raining", "hasn't slept" for "hadn't slept", "Hardly has she stepped…", "No sooner had … then …" (than/then), "did she realised" (the exact trap taught in the Not-until warn block), "did she realises". 43 of 65 one-word corruptions tried on this unit's gap/tr keys were accepted (shown to the student as "صحيح — انتبه للتهجئة" with the model answer, but scored as correct). Suggested fix for the owner: apply the typo tolerance only to `find` items, or only when the differing token is ≥5 letters and not a pure inflection change (-d/-s/-ed/-ing) and not in a stop-list (has/have/had/hand/was/were/where/did/does/than/then/when…).
2. "Hardly … than" and "No sooner … when" are attested in real usage; the keys (gr3 mcq[10], err[16], warn block) follow the traditional Cambridge/Tawjihi rule hardly/scarcely/barely → when, no sooner → than. Fine for the exam.
3. Distractors such as "talks he", "heard we", "realised we", "knew she" are archaic/literary inversions (wrong in modern standard English); kept because they are plausible learner errors.
4. Reading names (Wadi Zaytoun, Olive Terrace Hotel, Hala Qasem, …) are fictional; "Wadi Zaytoun" could coincide with a real wadi/locality name. No factual claims about real places are made (Jerash in the writing model is real and only mentioned as a site of ruins).
5. grammar[0].qs[5] ("After the story ____ online…") keys the past perfect (the only fitting option); past simple would be equally natural in real English, consistent with the unit's own note that past perfect is "expected" in narrative-tense exercises.
