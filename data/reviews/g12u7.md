# Review log — g12u7 "The creative urge" (independent review)

Work copy: data/wip/g12u7.review.js (original untouched in data/units until the final `mv`).

Method: every question (grammar 18+18+17, reading 11, test 20) was first re-solved blind (stems and options printed
without keys, answers written down, then compared with the file: 0 disagreements, so no answer key was wrong); every
mcq option was also tested as a possible second answer; every gap/tr list was checked against the app normaliser
(U.norm: lower-case, commas ignored, n't/'m/'re/'ve/'ll expanded, NOT 's/'d); every err token was checked (appears once,
single token, exactly one error); every order item was tested for a second natural arrangement (the grader accepts
exactly one sequence; the tokens keep their capital first letter and final full stop, which excludes e.g.
"She recommended the book that changed my life." and "In Zarqa lives the artist ..."); all English examples and Arabic
translations were read line by line twice; reading gist/tr/gloss checked paragraph by paragraph; word counts measured by
script (reading 445 words; model letter 225 words without salutation/sign-off, 231 with, range 200-250); inline markup
balance, Arabic typo/punctuation scan, vocab def/example length scan and answer-position spread (grammar and test)
were scripted. WebSearch used twice: (1) standard adjective order (sources disagree on shape vs age; none treats
pattern), (2) fronting of prepositions with phrasal verbs (learner convention confirmed).

## Who / whom / whoever / whomever audit
Every occurrence was listed and judged. Keyed items: "who" as subject (gr1 qs[0], qs[3]); "whom" only after a preposition
or after "of" (gr2 qs[4], qs[9], qs[11], err qs[15], order, tr qs[13]) — categorical, no debate; "whoever … scores"
(gr3 qs[2], key whoever, distractor whomever) and the warn "to whoever arrived first" — case is decided by the role
inside the relative clause, the standard prescriptive rule and also modern usage, so it was kept. No gap item has an
object who/whom without a preposition, so no `a` list needed both forms. The only debatable occurrence ("Whoever you
ask") was replaced. Informal "who" as stranded-preposition object appears only as a *correct but informal* option
(test[8]) and the key there is the formal "with whom", which is unambiguous.

## Comma / "that" audit
Non-defining items (gr1 qs[3], qs[5], qs[6], qs[9], qs[11], qs[15], gr2 qs[4], qs[9], qs[10], qs[11], test[5], test[7],
test[9]) all carry the commas the rule requires and every "that after a comma" is a distractor or an `err` target.
"That" is never keyed after a preposition, after a comma, for the whole-clause "which", or in a non-defining clause.

## Residual doubts
- Defining vs non-defining "implication" items (gr1 qs[5] "more than one brother", test[5] "ALL the students study hard") follow the
  textbook convention that a defining clause implies other members of the set; a purist can say it is only a strong
  implicature. Kept (standard exam convention).
- Warn "✗ The children after whom she looks": consistent with the learner rule that the particle of a phrasal/prepositional
  verb stays with the verb (the Arabic text says "غير طبيعي"); a few native speakers would tolerate it in very
  formal writing.
- Order items rely on the visible cues (capital first token, full stop on the last token) to exclude the alternatives noted
  above; a student who ignores the cues could build another grammatical sentence and be marked wrong. Judged acceptable.
- Originality: the reading title equals the title of the workbook text listed in data/scope.md ("The Many Aspects of
  Creativity"); a title alone is not copyable and the text, the four views, the sparrow poem and the model letter are
  original wording. The "one per cent inspiration" saying is a widely known proverb, presented as such.
- Shape-vs-age order inside the adjective chain is intentionally left unstated (the sources disagree).
