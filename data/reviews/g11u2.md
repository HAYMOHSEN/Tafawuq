## Fact check (animal facts) — all three animals verified against outside sources, no change needed in the passage
- Arabian oryx: hunted to extinction in the wild by 1972 (last wild animal shot in 1972) with cars and rifles; saved by captive-breeding
  herds (Phoenix Zoo "Operation Oryx" from 1962); IUCN: Endangered since 1986 → relisted Vulnerable in June 2011 (first species ever to
  move from Extinct in the Wild to Vulnerable); Shaumari Wildlife Reserve (RSCN, opened 1975, a few km from Azraq wetland, oryx
  breeding from 1978, releases from 1983). The passage's "By 1972 … wiped out", "2011 endangered → vulnerable", "Shaumari near Azraq" are correct.
- Sand cat: long dense hair on the soles insulates the pads against the extreme desert temperatures; gets most of its moisture from
  prey; hunts at night, rests in burrows by day; IUCN Least Concern (2016) with population size/trend unknown; habitat degradation is a
  main threat; recorded in Jordan (first sighting 1997). The passage's paws/water/nocturnal/"not classed as endangered worldwide"/"nobody
  knows how many" are correct.
- Syrian brown bear: the smallest and palest brown bear subspecies; mountain forest/meadow habitat; omnivorous; survives today in
  Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Iran, Iraq, Turkey and Turkmenistan; extirpated from Israel/Palestine/Jordan; extremely rare or absent in
  Syria; threats are forest loss and persecution where it damages beehives or livestock. The passage ("smaller and paler", "Levant",
  "mainly in Turkey, Iran, Iraq and the Caucasus", "raided beehives or farms") is correct.
