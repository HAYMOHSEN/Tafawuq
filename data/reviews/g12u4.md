## Residual doubts
1. gr2 qs[16] (order "Had it not been for her advice, I would have failed.") — in real English the conditional clause can also follow the main clause ("I would have failed had it not been for her advice"). The item is unambiguous only because the app shuffles raw tokens with punctuation attached ("Had" capitalised, "advice," / "failed."), so a clause-swapped answer cannot be built with correct punctuation. Kept; replace if the owner wants strict "one natural order".
2. gr1 qs[7] keys "less sugar … fewer biscuits" prescriptively (the unit says so openly); "less biscuits" is common in native speech. Likewise "neither of + plural" takes a singular verb in the key although BrE accepts both (the unit says "choose the singular in the exam").
3. Reading title "What tickles your taste buds?" and the three-writer A / B / C layout match the textbook lesson as recorded in data/scope.md. The passage itself is original (no shared sentences), but the owner may prefer a different title.
4. Several practice items reuse the lesson's example sentences almost verbatim (gr2 qs[0], qs[8], qs[10], qs[12], qs[11]), so they test recall more than rule application. Not wrong; could be varied later.
5. Writing model: "Provided that you book ahead, I cannot praise Olive Corner highly enough." is grammatical but logically a little odd (the praise seems to depend on booking). "Salt" (the city of As-Salt) in the title can be misread as the word "salt" by weaker students. test[18] says "formal" review while the unit calls the genre «شبه رسمي»; harmless (only one option is non-slang).
